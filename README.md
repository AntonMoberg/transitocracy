# Transitocracy prototype

Run the Godot project and use the **Roads / Trips / Zoning** selector above the chart. Each launch starts with a random state. **New map** generates a fresh seed; **From seed** recreates the seed shown in the header. The **Detail** control selects 32, 256, or 768 cities. The state initially simulates only 62, 286, or 798 neighborhoods respectively; new neighborhoods join as you approve road-frontage land. Every setting uses the same roughly 400,000 km² footprint, comparable to [California's land area](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/CA). Reusing a seed at another detail setting preserves the coastline and distances. Generation runs on a worker so the interface remains responsive.

The road map stays visible while you inspect the state, regions, counties, cities, and developed neighborhoods. Colored boundaries and labels mark regions, counties, and cities. Zoom into a city to see its small neighborhood cells; most municipal land starts empty. Click a visible neighborhood or its gateway to inspect it. Click an area label or boundary, or select a name in the right inspector, to see its finances, policies, and children without changing the map. The breadcrumb above the inspector returns to a parent area. Tax and fee controls appear only for the selected area; directional toll controls appear when a road is selected.

## Zoning

Switch to **Zoning** and zoom in until road-frontage cells appear. Amber cells await approval, green cells are approved, and gray cells have lost road access. Click a cell to open its details, then choose **Approve development**. Approval adds a neighborhood to the economic and flow simulation; residents and jobs begin arriving on daily updates if the arterial remains connected. **Withdraw approval** stops positive growth while keeping existing homes, jobs, and residents. Land-use mix and density are generated defaults for now and are shown before approval; later zoning controls can be added to this panel. Completed connected arterials create candidates along their routes. Highways alone do not create frontage.

## Road tools

- **Inspect:** click a road for its directional vehicle demand, queue, capacity, travel time and upkeep. Click a neighborhood gateway for its statistics.
- **Arterial:** click a start node, optional empty map points to shape the route, then an end node. Review the cost and opening date, then press **Build**.
- **Highway:** draw the same way, using active interchanges at both ends.
- **Junction:** click empty map to add a road node, or click an arterial to split it and connect future roads there.
- **Interchange:** click a node or a highway segment, then press **Build**. Highway exits become usable when construction finishes.
- **Widen:** click an open road, then press **Build** to add one lane in each direction. Existing lanes remain open during construction.
- **Demolish:** click an open road, review the resulting number of disconnected neighborhood groups, then press **Demolish**. Removal is immediate, has no refund, and ends that road's upkeep. Roads with construction underway must finish first.

Right click or press Escape to clear a road preview. On a MacBook trackpad, **pinch to zoom** and **swipe with two fingers to pan**; Command-scroll also zooms. A mouse wheel zooms and dragging pans. Zoom stays anchored at the pointer, and all detail settings support close neighborhood inspection. The map's **− / + / Fit** buttons offer another way to navigate; Fit restores the whole state. Street zoom reaches 10 pixels per meter; highway lanes, medians, and shoulders and arterial lanes then use physical widths, while broader views keep roads legible. The scale bar shows real map distances. The **Trips** view retains the work, commercial and social flow arrows. Space pauses; keys **1–4** choose Second, Minute, Hour, or Day simulation. **Q/W/E** choose Normal, Faster (2×), or Fastest (4×) pace. The clock caps its actual rate when calculations fall behind and shows the measured rate beside the controls.

Starter and player-built roads follow deterministic, gently curved corridors. Highway approaches start and end with a short straight tangent, up to 100 m, so through roads meet at zero degrees; coastal detours move inland when a shoreline route would force a sharp bend. Generated highways target a minimum 2 km bend radius, and player-built highway previews reject routes below that limit. Player waypoints remain exact anchors; routes detour around water. The preview shows the actual road shape, and its length determines travel time, construction cost, and upkeep. Highways inside one region are funded by that region; highways crossing regions are funded by the state. Arterials belong to the smallest jurisdiction covering their full route. Regions start with a $300 million road reserve and receive daily state grants for regional road upkeep plus an equal share of 20% of state income tax revenue. The state may enter debt to pay these grants. A city funds a standalone junction interchange; a highway exit follows its highway's owner. Tolls belong to the road owner. Construction takes roughly one tenth of the original project duration, rounded up to full days: a short arterial takes at least 2 days, a highway at least 5 days, and an interchange or highway exit takes 5 days. Each road direction uses the right-hand side and has its own group of lines, one per lane. Color shows served flow versus capacity or a remaining queue; arrows show travel direction. Dashed lines are construction projects.

## Simulation modes

| Mode | Base game time per real second | Traffic calculation | Available while inspecting |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Second | 1 second | Directional road flow and queues updated each second | Every level |
| Minute | 1 minute | Directional roads advanced in short batches | Every level |
| Hour | 1 hour | City-to-city demand assigned to road corridors | State or region |
| Day | 1 day | Daily average region-to-region demand assigned to road corridors | State or region |

Every mode uses the same calendar, directional queue totals, daily trip profiles, and neighborhood economy. Work, shopping, and social trips follow the existing hourly departure distributions. The fast modes estimate traffic on the existing road network: highways and arterials crossing city boundaries retain directional congestion colors, while neighborhood links and nodes are subdued. Planned major roads keep their actual construction geometry. Selecting a county, city, or neighborhood during Hour or Day mode returns to Minute mode at the same timestamp and reconstructs plausible local queues from the aggregate total. The simulation preserves population and jurisdiction finances during mode changes.

Road flow is aggregate vehicle counts rather than individual agents. Each direction exposes arrival rate, served rate, queue size, and speed so a later renderer can place cosmetic cars along the right-hand lanes. The clock advances only through completed calculations. Day mode prepares seven upcoming days as one continuous background cycle and keeps a bounded buffer of daily results, so the calendar can pass midnight without a separate calculation pause each day. Its timeline shows seven calendar days with daily work, commercial, social, or combined trip totals; dim points are estimates until the background calculation prepares those days. Click a day to see its total. Other modes keep the 24-hour timeline. Road openings, taxes, and neighborhood migration still take effect on their scheduled dates. Changing a policy or road discards prepared future days and starts a fresh cycle. The fastest pace is a target; playback follows the measured preparation rate when CPU time is limited.

The small map generates one state, four regions, eight counties, 32 cities, and 62 initially simulated neighborhoods. The default map generates 16 regions, 64 counties, 256 cities, and 286 initial neighborhoods. The stretch map has 768 cities and 798 initial neighborhoods. Every city has a small seed cell; ten starting cities have four populated cells each. More neighborhood cells appear along completed arterials and enter the simulation when approved. County counts vary by region, and city counts vary by county.

Each seed procedurally generates an elongated, flat coastal state with large bays, smaller coves, headlands, and straight inland borders. State length is approximately 1,250–1,500 km. Regions, counties, and cities fully subdivide their parent areas. Neighborhoods occupy small, varied polygons with shared straight edges; most have five to seven sides, while some have four. Their area is typically around 2–3 km² and capped near 4 km²; cells clipped by city borders can be smaller. Most city land remains undeveloped. Shared administrative borders use varied angles and irregular bends; coastal districts follow the shoreline. City settlement points are chosen inside their boundaries rather than fixed at the polygon centroid. The boundary splitter favors compact cities, and starting highways detour around bays. Labels avoid overlaps and neighborhood detail appears only when zoomed in far enough to read it.

Ten geographically distributed cities start with **four developed neighborhoods each**. Their hubs have interchanges and are joined by a sparse tree of gently curved two-lane highways. One or two arterial trunks leave each starting hub; the developed neighborhoods join those trunks through local junctions and branches. Two highways through a city hub share a continuous tangent; at larger junctions, the best-aligned pair forms the through route and other highways branch from it. New highways continue an existing single highway smoothly. Initial population is capped at 100,000. Other city seed cells are dormant, and most land has no neighborhood object until an arterial exposes a candidate. Candidate land can remain empty indefinitely if the player does not approve it.

A neighborhood begins developing after both **zoning approval** and a completed road route to a starting-city hub. Connecting a road without approval, building an isolated local road, or funding an unfinished project does not activate land. Growth slows smoothly with distance from the city center, so remote approved land remains possible but develops on a much longer timescale. Affordability and job access also affect growth. If an inhabited neighborhood loses its connection, residents and jobs decline gradually while trips within the remaining road component continue. Reconnection resumes growth if approval remains in effect.

Road crossings connect only at explicit junctions or interchanges. Detailed terrain, local streets, transit services, and rendered cars are later layers.

Large maps use compact city and region origin–destination demand. Work trips balance workers and jobs over each city's 64 nearest reachable cities; shopping and social trips use the same candidate set with gravity weights. The hourly Gaussian departure curves still drive Second, Minute, and Hour modes. Day mode uses one daily average road assignment on the existing links, retaining directional congestion estimates and fiscal totals. Map rendering caps visible neighborhood detail and hides local roads at broad state scale. A severed neighborhood gateway loses road access and external trips. A worker prepares the next seven days and copies state snapshots outside the UI thread; large-map road edits also recalculate on a worker.

## Verification

The `tests/` directory contains headless smoke checks for generation, trip flow, migration, UI, road construction, traffic, multiscale modes, day handoffs, and parallel simulation consistency. `tests/state_development_smoke.gd` checks ten anchors, the starting population cap, irregular candidate lookup, construction-gated candidate creation, approval, withdrawal, and detached worker snapshots. `tests/city_shape_smoke.gd` checks compact city borders, offset settlement points, and shared neighborhood edges. `tests/zoning_ui_smoke.gd` exercises map selection, the approval panel, and its async rebuild. `tests/organic_geography_smoke.gd` checks administrative tiling and contained neighborhood cells. `tests/map_navigation_smoke.gd` checks camera controls. Run `tests/zoom_render_benchmark.gd` to measure drawing across zoom levels, and `tests/large_scale_benchmark.gd` to measure simulation performance.
