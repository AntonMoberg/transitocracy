# Transitocracy prototype

Run the Godot project and use the **Roads / Trips / Zoning** selector above the chart. Each launch starts with a random state. **New map** generates a fresh seed; **From seed** recreates the seed shown in the header. The **Detail** control selects 32, 256, or 768 cities. The state initially simulates only 62, 286, or 798 neighborhoods respectively; new neighborhoods join as you approve road-frontage land. Every setting uses the same roughly 400,000 km² footprint, comparable to [California's land area](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/CA). Reusing a seed at another detail setting preserves the coastline and distances. Generation runs on a worker so the interface remains responsive.

The road map stays visible while you inspect the state, regions, counties, cities, and developed neighborhoods. Colored boundaries and labels mark regions, counties, and cities. Zoom into a city to see connected arterials, collectors, local streets, blocks, parks, and flat building footprints; most municipal land starts empty. Neighborhood boundaries follow the generated street layout rather than a road spur to a central dot. Click a neighborhood, street, or jurisdiction to inspect it. Click an area label or boundary, or select a name in the right inspector, to see its finances, policies, and children without changing the map. The breadcrumb above the inspector returns to a parent area. Tax and fee controls appear only for the selected area; directional toll controls appear when a road is selected.

## Zoning

Switch to **Zoning** and zoom in until road-frontage plans appear. Amber plans await approval, green plans are approved, and gray plans have lost road access. Selecting a plan previews its street mesh and estimated homes, jobs, infrastructure cost, and required top-up. Completed connected arterials expose plans along their routes; highways alone do not create frontage. Layouts follow the arterial direction, clip to city boundaries and existing street districts, and have multiple collector connections instead of a single central access road.

Choose a city-funded allocation and **Approve development**. Money moves from the city's available treasury into the plan's reserved funds; approval does not open roads or create residents immediately. Public collectors and local streets open in three construction phases, servicing approximately 35%, 70%, and 100% of the target capacity. Each paid phase takes two game days. Expansion waits until roughly 60% of the currently serviced homes and jobs are occupied, and pauses if funds or road access are missing. Existing serviced capacity can continue filling while expansion awaits funds. Use **Top up** or **Return** to adjust unspent allocations. Completed plans return excess reserves automatically. Road upkeep starts when infrastructure opens and remains payable after cancellation.

Open **Budget, land use and density** to set Residential, Commercial, Office, Mixed, and Industry shares, totaling 100%, with Low, Medium, or High density for each. Draft zoning can be included in the initial approval. Later edits apply at the next daily boundary, with a preview of target capacity and additional street funding. Higher demand can require a separately paid, two-day capacity upgrade; it does not grant free extra lanes. These are prototype public-infrastructure costs, not a charge for privately constructed homes or offices. Buildings appear gradually as occupancy grows. Downzoning or cancellation does not instantly remove existing residents, jobs, or building footprints.

**Withdraw approval** cancels further expansion and positive growth. It refunds only unspent reserves; money already spent is not refunded, paid construction finishes, and existing streets and buildings remain. Neighborhood inspectors also link back to their funding and zoning controls. Fiscal labels distinguish available treasury from reserved development funds.

## Road tools

- **Inspect:** click a major road for directional demand, queues, capacity, travel time, and upkeep. At street zoom, click a local street for its assigned flow and queue, or select a neighborhood for its statistics and development controls.
- **Arterial:** click a start node, optional empty map points to shape the route, then an end node. Review the cost and opening date, then press **Build**.
- **Highway:** draw the same way, using active interchanges at both ends.
- **Junction:** click empty map to add a road node, or click an arterial or collector to split it and connect future roads there.
- **Interchange:** click a node or a highway segment, then press **Build**. Highway exits become usable when construction finishes.
- **Widen:** click an open player-managed road, then press **Build** to add one lane in each direction. Existing lanes remain open during construction. Generated collectors and local streets are managed through their development plan, not individually widened or demolished.
- **Demolish:** click an open road, review the resulting number of disconnected neighborhood groups, then press **Demolish**. Removal is immediate, has no refund, and ends that road's upkeep. Roads with construction underway must finish first.

Right click or press Escape to clear a road preview. On a MacBook trackpad, **pinch to zoom** and **swipe with two fingers to pan**; Command-scroll also zooms. A mouse wheel zooms and dragging pans. Zoom stays anchored at the pointer, and all detail settings support close neighborhood inspection. The map's **− / + / Fit** buttons offer another way to navigate; Fit restores the whole state. Street zoom reaches 10 pixels per meter; highway lanes, medians, and shoulders and arterial lanes then use physical widths, while broader views keep roads legible. The scale bar shows real map distances. The **Trips** view retains the work, commercial and social flow arrows. Space pauses; keys **1–4** choose Second, Minute, Hour, or Day simulation. **Q/W/E** choose Normal, Faster (2×), or Fastest (4×) pace. The clock caps its actual rate when calculations fall behind and shows the measured rate beside the controls.

Player-built corridors and starter highways follow deterministic, gently curved routes; starter urban arterials form sparse, straight through corridors. Highway approaches start and end with a short straight tangent, up to 100 m, so through roads meet at zero degrees; coastal detours move inland when a shoreline route would force a sharp bend. Generated highways target a minimum 2 km bend radius, and player-built highway previews reject routes below that limit. Player waypoints remain exact anchors; routes detour around water. The preview shows the actual road shape, and its length determines travel time, construction cost, and upkeep. Highways inside one region are funded by that region; highways crossing regions are funded by the state. Arterials belong to the smallest jurisdiction covering their full route. Regions start with a $300 million road reserve and receive daily state grants for regional road upkeep plus an equal share of 20% of state income tax revenue. The state may enter debt to pay these grants. A city funds a standalone junction interchange; a highway exit follows its highway's owner. Tolls belong to the road owner. Construction takes roughly one tenth of the original project duration, rounded up to full days: a short arterial takes at least 2 days, a highway at least 5 days, and an interchange or highway exit takes 5 days. Each road direction uses the right-hand side and has its own group of lines, one per lane. Color shows served flow versus capacity or a remaining queue; arrows show travel direction. Dashed lines are construction projects.

## Simulation modes

| Mode | Base game time per real second | Traffic calculation | Available while inspecting |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Second | 1 second | Directional road flow and queues updated each second | Every level |
| Minute | 1 minute | Directional roads advanced in short batches | Every level |
| Hour | 1 hour | City-to-city demand assigned to road corridors | State or region |
| Day | 1 day | Daily average region-to-region demand assigned to road corridors | State or region |

Every mode uses the same calendar, directional queue totals, daily trip profiles, and neighborhood economy. Work, shopping, and social trips follow the existing hourly departure distributions. The fast modes estimate traffic on the existing road network: highways and arterials crossing city boundaries retain directional congestion colors, while neighborhood links and nodes are subdued. Planned major roads keep their actual construction geometry. Selecting a county, city, or neighborhood during Hour or Day mode returns to Minute mode at the same timestamp and reconstructs plausible local queues from the aggregate total. The simulation preserves population and jurisdiction finances during mode changes.

Road flow is aggregate vehicle counts rather than individual agents. Each direction exposes arrival rate, served rate, queue size, and speed so a later renderer can place cosmetic cars along the right-hand lanes. The clock advances only through completed calculations. Day mode prepares seven upcoming days as one continuous background cycle and keeps a bounded buffer of daily results, so the calendar can pass midnight without a separate calculation pause each day. Its timeline shows seven calendar days with daily work, commercial, social, or combined trip totals; dim points are estimates until the background calculation prepares those days. Click a day to see its total. Other modes keep the 24-hour timeline. Road openings, taxes, and neighborhood migration still take effect on their scheduled dates. Changing a policy or road discards prepared future days and starts a fresh cycle. The fastest pace is a target; playback follows the measured preparation rate when CPU time is limited.

The small map generates one state, four regions, eight counties, 32 cities, and 62 initially simulated neighborhoods. The default map generates 16 regions, 64 counties, 256 cities, and 286 initial neighborhoods. The stretch map has 768 cities and 798 initial neighborhoods. Every city has a small seed cell; ten starting cities have four populated street districts each. More neighborhood plans appear along completed arterials and enter the simulation when approved. County counts vary by region, and city counts vary by county.

Each seed procedurally generates an elongated, flat coastal state with large bays, smaller coves, headlands, and straight inland borders. State length is approximately 1,250–1,500 km. Regions, counties, and cities fully subdivide their parent areas. Dormant seed cells retain the small administrative polygons. Developed neighborhoods instead mix rectangular, tapered, skewed, and multi-sided road-led footprints, typically around 1–2 km². Adjacent districts agree on shared boundary geometry without requiring an arterial on every side. Local blocks vary in width and length, with angled quadrilaterals and occasional triangular sites along connected diagonal streets; some districts retain conventional straight grids. Buildings are set back and clipped to their actual plots, and local demand is weighted by block area. Most city land remains undeveloped. Shared administrative borders use varied angles and irregular bends; coastal districts follow the shoreline. City settlement points are chosen inside their boundaries rather than fixed at the polygon centroid. The boundary splitter favors compact cities, and starting highways detour around bays. Labels avoid overlaps and neighborhood detail appears only when zoomed in far enough to read it.

Ten geographically distributed cities start with **four developed neighborhoods each**. Their hubs have interchanges and are joined by a sparse tree of gently curved two-lane highways. Long, straight arterial spines cross at the starting interchange and continue for several kilometers across neighborhood boundaries, rather than tracing a bent arterial ring around every district. Collector streets shape the remaining irregular district edges and connect the local block streets to these through routes. Larger developed areas add parallel arterials, not an arterial on every neighborhood boundary. Their surrounding municipal land remains undeveloped. Two highways through a city hub share a continuous tangent; at larger junctions, the best-aligned pair forms the through route and other highways branch from it. New highways continue an existing single highway smoothly. Initial population is capped at 100,000. Other city seed cells are dormant, and most land has no neighborhood object until an arterial exposes a candidate. Candidate land can remain empty indefinitely if the player does not approve it.

A neighborhood begins developing after both **zoning approval** and a completed road route to a starting-city hub. Connecting a road without approval, building an isolated local road, or funding an unfinished project does not activate land. Growth slows smoothly with distance from the city center, so remote approved land remains possible but develops on a much longer timescale. Affordability and job access also affect growth. If an inhabited neighborhood loses its connection, residents and jobs decline gradually while trips within the remaining road component continue. Reconnection resumes growth if approval remains in effect.

New surface arterials connect automatically where they cross existing arterials or collectors. Local access is organized through collector junctions rather than connecting every minor street directly to a major corridor. Highway crossings remain grade-separated unless an interchange is built. Terrain, transit services, individual vehicle agents, and rendered cars remain future layers.

Large maps use compact city and region origin–destination demand. Work trips balance workers and jobs over each city's 64 nearest reachable cities; shopping and social trips use the same candidate set with gravity weights. The hourly Gaussian departure curves still drive Second, Minute, and Hour modes. Day mode uses one daily average road assignment on the existing links, retaining directional congestion estimates and fiscal totals. Map rendering caps visible neighborhood detail and hides local roads at broad state scale. A neighborhood loses external access only when all of its usable connections are severed; removing one street need not disconnect an entire district. A worker prepares the next seven days and copies state snapshots outside the UI thread; large-map road edits also recalculate on a worker.

## Street simulation architecture

- `street_layout_generator.gd` produces seeded, immutable street geometry, phase costs, street-defined blocks, and building footprints. `street_network.gd` installs only completed collector infrastructure into the main graph, reusing and splitting real intersections.
- `development_plan.gd` and `development_budget.gd` keep mutable allocations, spending, construction, zoning, and serviced-capacity limits separate from geometry. Worker snapshots detach this state while sharing immutable layouts.
- `urban_routing.gd` routes through per-city arterial/collector graphs and multiple neighborhood access points. Regional searches omit city interiors that cannot provide a through route; cities with multiple external portals retain their connecting streets. Same-city trips use actual city paths, not a mandatory central hub.
- `local_street_traffic.gd` keeps dense local graphs and directional fluid queues separate from the statewide road graph. Land-use-weighted neighborhood demand loads real local edges, with capacity-limited discharge, persistent queues, hourly alternatives when queued, and access-delay feedback. Local link-entry/discharge accounting is conserved. This is hierarchical aggregate traffic, not individual door-to-door driver simulation.
- Local street queues advance independently of camera zoom and simulation mode. Worker forks, forecast rewinds, and daily handoffs retain detached queue state. `urban_map_layer.gd` culls distant districts and draws local streets, parks, and flat buildings only at useful zoom levels; opening the map does not create simulation infrastructure.

## Verification

The `tests/` directory contains headless smoke checks for generation, trip flow, migration, UI, road construction, traffic, multiscale modes, day handoffs, and parallel simulation consistency. `tests/state_development_smoke.gd` checks ten anchors, the starting population cap, irregular candidate lookup, construction-gated candidate creation, approval, withdrawal, and detached worker snapshots. `tests/city_shape_smoke.gd` checks compact city borders, offset settlement points, and shared neighborhood edges. `tests/zoning_ui_smoke.gd` exercises map selection, the approval panel, and its async rebuild. `tests/organic_geography_smoke.gd` checks administrative tiling and contained neighborhood cells. `tests/map_navigation_smoke.gd` checks camera controls. `tests/urban_development_smoke.gd` checks phased funding, cancellation, serviced capacity, rezoning, paid upgrades, retained buildings, and snapshot isolation. `tests/urban_traffic_smoke.gd` checks congested local-flow conservation, batching, mode changes, alternative routes, and forecast checkpoints. `tests/urban_junction_smoke.gd` checks real surface intersections and highway grade separation. `tests/urban_generation_smoke.gd` checks fourteen seeded states across all map sizes. `tests/zoning_scale_benchmark.gd -- --map-index` builds over 5,000 fully streeted districts, reports the actual fine-edge count, and measures snapshots, daily updates, and warm map drawing. Run `tests/zoom_render_benchmark.gd` to measure drawing across zoom levels, and `tests/large_scale_benchmark.gd` to measure simulation performance.
