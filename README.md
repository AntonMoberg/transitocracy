# Transitocracy prototype

Open the project in Godot 4.7.2 Mono and run `scenes/main.tscn`. **New map** makes a random state; **From seed** recreates the current seed. **Detail** selects 32, 256, or 768 cities. The same seed preserves regional geography across detail settings. Generation and large road edits run on workers so the map remains responsive.

Terrain gives each state broad plains, long mountain ranges with foothills and high rocky crests, winding lakes, connected rivers, bays, and coves. Drainage also shapes fine valley detail. City sites and road corridors favor gentler ground. The starting network includes sparse regional arterials connecting additional settlements, with surface junctions and grade-separated highway crossings. **Map** uses pale mint open land, soft green woods, cream dry ground, sand exposed rock and subtle relief. Regional roads appear as thin route lines; lanes and arrows appear closer in. Background workers sharpen only visible tiles as you zoom in. On Map, **Terrain** adds shaded relief; **Slope** and **Contours** reveal grades and elevation lines. **Traffic** colors roads by congestion and **Transit** shows planned rail corridors and station sites. The same seed remains repeatable, although maps generated by earlier versions may look different.

Use **Roads / Trips / Zoning** above the chart and turn map layers on or off with the buttons on the map. Click a state, region, county, city, neighborhood, or street to inspect it. Zoom into a city to see collectors, local streets, blocks, parks, and buildings. Space pauses simulation; keys **1–4** select Second, Minute, Hour, or Day time steps. **Q/W/E** select playback speed.

## City growth

Each city has a persistent street lattice with stable junction and block addresses. Starting neighborhoods and future proposals claim varied, connected groups of blocks instead of fixed rectangles. Approving one never shifts an adjacent district's streets. Perimeter streets and through collectors align at shared addresses. A diagonal arterial can gradually turn the grid in nearby undeveloped land; built junctions stay fixed. Local block patterns vary by city and by the design selected for a proposal.

Open **Zoning** to see suggested districts. Amber dots show proposals at state scale; zoom in for outlines and a street preview. The review list ranks connected proposals. Select several entries and use **Approve selected projects** to fund them together, or click a district for a detailed plan. Development always needs player approval. A disconnected arterial or highway alone does not create development.

The plan editor offers **Fine grid**, **Longer blocks across**, and **Longer blocks along**. These change block lengths while preserving shared junctions and through streets. Choose a neighborhood **Character** for parcel frontages and building footprints. Set Residential, Commercial, Office, Mixed, and Industry shares and their densities in **Budget, land use and density**. City inspectors provide Growth, Blocks, Land use, and Density policies for future proposals. Existing developed blocks keep their approved design.

Approving a project reserves city money. Collectors and local streets open in construction phases, increasing serviced capacity from 35% to 70% to 100%. Growth waits for access, funds, and demand. **Top up** and **Return** adjust unspent reserves. **Withdraw approval** stops further growth and returns unspent funds; completed roads and occupied buildings remain. **Dev Mode** provides unlimited funding without changing treasury balances.

New districts bend their streets gently on hillsides and omit local streets that would climb too sharply. Lakes and slopes above 25% stay free of ordinary buildings; the usable land within a district determines its capacity.

## Road and planning tools

- **Arterial** and **Highway** draw Straight, Curved, or Multi-point Curved routes. Start on a road or node, place curve guides when needed, and choose an end. Outside developed city streets, a two-road connection follows the existing approach tangent. Double click empty land to stage an arterial extension; extend its endpoint after it opens. Highway connections include their required interchanges in the preview and price.
- **Snap 5°** uses the turn relative to the road being extended. **Snap 5 m** rounds segment length. The preview shows construction cost, required land acquisition, demolition, and local street work. At a crossing, choose **Above** or **Below**; a grade-separated crossing remains disconnected until an interchange is built.
- The road preview also marks and prices bridges, tunnels, and grading across difficult terrain. Tunnel sections acquire land only at their surface approaches.
- **Growth corridor** records a two-point city planning guide and raises the priority of nearby development proposals. **Rail corridor** records a future rail alignment; **Station site** marks a future station location and raises nearby growth priority. These are planning inputs; rail service and trains are not simulated yet.
- **Junction** splits an existing surface road, **Interchange** adds highway access, **Widen** adds a lane per direction, and **Demolish** removes an open road after showing its connectivity impact.

New arterials and highways through developed land acquire and clear affected blocks and pay for site work and local street reconstruction. Replacement parcels fit around the new road when it opens; unaffected parcels retain their zoning. A surface arterial preserves selected, spaced local street crossings and trims the others along their existing heading. Highway clearance prevents buildings beneath the highway, including elevated segments. Surface roads connect at intersections; highway crossings require interchanges. Right click or press Escape to clear an unfinished route or planning guide.

Road connection previews run on a worker. Green stretches reuse an existing corridor; dashed stretches show construction. Funding reserves the priced work, while the existing roads stay open until the replacement branches and junctions open together. A changed network invalidates an unfunded preview. Roads involved in a funded connection cannot be demolished or widened while it is under construction.

## Simulation and code

The simulation uses aggregate directional traffic and queues, city and neighborhood economies, phased budgets, and worker snapshots. Second and Minute modes run local traffic; Hour and Day modes use compact regional assignments on the same built road network. Map detail is culled by zoom and viewport.

- `urban_street_graph.gd` owns city street addresses, deterministic block clusters, designs, and graph edge status. `graph_growth_planner.gd` ranks adjacent districts using built access, city policies, and planning guides. `development_planner.gd` registers and approves proposals; `development_budget.gd` opens paid street phases.
- `street_network.gd` installs collectors into the physical road graph and reuses existing junctions. `street_layout_ops.gd` supplies shared street geometry; `parcel_geometry.gd` creates building parcels. `road_land.gd` handles road clearance, acquisition, demolition, and local redevelopment.
- `region_snapshot.gd` detaches mutable simulation state for workers. `map_chunk_renderer.gd` manages retained GPU chunks and traffic colors; `map_geometry.gd` prepares indexed, simplified geometry on workers. `map_terrain_layer.gd` retains terrain tiles and coarse fallback coverage. `region_map.gd` handles navigation, picking, labels, suggestions, and construction previews; `urban_map_layer.gd` retains local street picking and preview helpers.
- `terrain_generator.gd` derives seeded elevation, drainage, lakes, and rivers; `terrain_data.gd` provides shared read-only terrain queries. `road_terrain.gd` classifies and prices road structures.

## Map rendering

The map uses world-space GPU meshes organized into a quadtree with nominal 512-pixel tiles. Camera motion transforms existing meshes immediately; two shared background workers prepare visible terrain and geometry. Roads simplify at distant zooms while retaining junction vertices. Solid colored strokes remain readable until physical lanes and arrows are large enough to resolve; local streets, parcels, and building shadows appear progressively. Visible tiles at the destination zoom are requested immediately, with roads and city blocks ahead of offscreen prefetch. A loaded ancestor stays visible until replacement coverage is ready; intermediate zoom levels are not prerequisites. Small geometry jobs are batched, and terrain can occupy at most one of the two workers. New revisions replace a chunk only after all its mesh parts have uploaded; stale worker results are discarded.

Main-thread publication has a soft 1 ms budget shared by geometry and terrain. Meshes split at 12,288 vertices so a single large feature cannot monopolize an upload. The geometry cache targets 192 MiB and evicts unused chunks incrementally; terrain retains up to 160 tiles. These are cache limits, not a bound on total process memory: snapshots, in-flight results, terrain source data, and driver allocations also consume memory. Driver upload calls are indivisible, so the time budget is a scheduling target rather than a hard real-time guarantee.

Traffic uses a compact directional color texture updated at most 15 times per second, interpolated in the road shader. Changing traffic, selection, or overlays does not regenerate road geometry. Feature fingerprints restrict geometry invalidation to affected layers and tiles. Labels, planning previews, and small interactive overlays still use Godot's canvas drawing API.

Map opens with Traffic and Terrain off. The base palette uses pale mint open land, soft green woods, cream dry ground, sand exposed rock, off-white developed blocks and blue water. Land cover uses the same world-space height/vegetation sampling at every tile level; Map relief is limited to ±6% brightness. Terrain adds stronger relief.

The default road inspection view shows developed blocks, roads and place labels without neighborhood statistics circles or junction markers. Neighborhood circles appear in Trips analysis; road-node targets appear when a road editing tool is active. Selection outlines and road previews remain available.

Zoning suggestions draw on the live interactive overlay, using the same camera as picking. They appear as overview dots or district outlines at closer zooms; selected suggestions show their street preview. They are not part of the static zoom-band bake. `zoning_map_gpu_smoke.gd` checks rendered visibility across zooms, offset cameras, selection and switching back to the normal map; `zoning_ui_smoke.gd` checks candidate approval through the full UI.

All terrain layers, including the whole-state fallback, use meshes clipped to the actual land polygon. Texture alpha no longer defines the coastline, so coarse pixels cannot bleed into the ocean behind a sharper tile. Map uploads half-float vegetation/dryness/rock fields with shared edge gutters; the GPU classifies these into crisp color regions with a screen-pixel antialias fringe. Visible Map tiles target at most two screen pixels per field sample. Refinement samples vegetation at fixed world positions and caches broad height derivatives; it does not change simulation terrain or coast geometry. Plain Map tiles skip relief and analysis raster generation. Enabling those layers requests missing raster data while retaining available coverage.

Road classes carry independent cartographic colors and live shader visibility. With the 110-pixel scale bar, local streets appear at 500 m and closer (110–121 pixels/km fade), urban collectors at 1 km (55–60.5 pixels/km), and ordinary rural/arterial routes fade between 5 km and 10 km (11–22 pixels/km). Highways and the static settlement-access backbone remain visible at overview. River thresholds use width: 60 m at 1 km, 160 m at 2–5 km, and 240 m at 10 km and farther. Picking follows the same policy; selected roads remain visible. Completed-block land-cover skirts close street-width gaps without painting undeveloped jurisdictions.

### Initial road network

New worlds choose complete terrain-weighted state corridors between developed starting cities before refining their highway geometry. Both map axes are considered; long crossings target at least 70% of settlement extent on an axis. A second primary crossing must serve additional cities and have a validated connection to the first. Regional branches attach to actual interior interchanges. Highway gateways are independent of rural-road sampling.

Routing favors low relative elevation and limited climbing, tries broad surface bends before tighter legal curves, then refines difficult sections in bounded windows. Surface passes precede exceptional tunnels (3 km each, at most 5% of a highway). Final grade, curve, land and bridge checks still apply. Route-specific avoidance is not stored in reusable terrain-edge costs, and changing road class invalidates grade-dependent costs.

The countryside samples at 30 km spacing, with optional-loop degree capped at three and a 1.85× detour threshold. This was calibrated from an initial 32 km setting to preserve a modest reduction. Connectivity uses the actual graph, including highway interior junctions; city access and overview importance are resolved after the whole network is built. Existing worlds receive the new rendering style; road geography changes on regeneration.

Generation consolidates parallel corridors when they stay within 1 km and 20 degrees for at least 5 km. Already-connected approaches also merge over short shared stretches (at least 100 m within 25 m), moving the fork to the divergence after unused branches are pruned. Highways take precedence over arterials, then rural roads. Bridge and tunnel stretches are excluded. Unused rural tails and duplicate return branches are removed, and an unused highway tail stops at its final interchange. Degree-two joins outside actual developed streets are rounded together without moving unrelated junctions; isolated sharp vertices within a road are rounded where terrain and junction constraints allow. City connections favor gates facing the incoming route to avoid looping around backward-facing exits. Rendering preserves endpoint approach vertices through LOD simplification. Existing saved networks are not automatically rewritten.

`road_topology_smoke.gd` and `road_compound_smoke.gd` cover continuous joins, shared corridors, priced interchange opening, staged extensions, detached snapshots, and stale previews. `road_network_metrics.gd` checks connectivity, physical attachments, dead ends and two-arm continuity across three seeds at 256 and 768 cities.

This follows the worker layout / retained GPU buffer architecture documented by [Mapbox GL JS](https://github.com/mapbox/mapbox-gl-js/blob/main/ARCHITECTURE.md). It applies the techniques of modern vector maps inside Godot; it does not embed a map service or depend on proprietary Google/Apple internals.

GPU readback checks run outside the measured section. The benchmark covers map navigation after initial loading, not world generation or simulation throughput. `large_ui_smoke.gd` separately exercises day-mode handoff in the full UI.
